# Home page read at v0.1.2356, 375 and 1280 px (2026-11-27T00:50Z)

Asked by cl-6.correspondent, relaying Dinis. Three SYNTHETIC readers, read cold, each their own view and none a real person's: a Portuguese tech reader on a phone (375 px), a founder on a desktop (1280 px), a journalist on a desktop. Method: scripted Chromium, full-page screenshots (kept in my scratchpad, not in the repository), page text and positions. No sideways scroll at either width. I did not open the stories.

## The five points asked
1. **The lead reads as today's main headline: yes, with one weakness.** "Portugal's tech investment is set to enter Europe's top ten" is the only large headline, in a dark band, with the lead sentence and a clear button. Its date is the word "Yesterday" in small green type on dark green, easy to miss and low in contrast. A cold reader on the morning of 27 Nov reads it as today's news. The byline reads "the 6.journalist desk, an AI agent, for Dinis Cruz": "6.journalist" looks like a leaked internal name (cl-6) and means nothing to a reader.
2. **Dates are on almost everything, but in three styles.** The lead says "Yesterday", the numbered list says "Yesterday" or "23 Nov", the Algarve cards and stories say "16 Nov" or "17 Nov". All have a date; the mix of relative and absolute means a reader cannot compare them (is "Yesterday" newer than "23 Nov"?). The town cards' "16 Nov" sits under the blurb in small bold type and reads as a label, not as the date of the story under it.
3. **The Algarve town grid against the map.** Desktop: balanced in height (map about 640 px, grid about 780 px). The grid shows 9 of 17 towns with "All 17 towns" below; the map shows 35 unlabelled green dots, so a reader cannot tell which dot is Faro or Lagos, and the Algarve cluster at the bottom is a smudge of 15 dots with one small label. The grid's order is not alphabetical and not by story count (Faro has 28, comes second). Every shown town has a story (11 Nov to 16 Nov), but none is newer than 16 Nov, so "Now in the Algarve" feels old. The legend counts "dated news this week (0)" and "today or yesterday (0)" read as broken, not as honest. "11 stories . 12 on the AI map" is not clear to a cold reader (what is the AI map?). Several blurbs end in cut-off words ("research (a...", "the cork industry and..."). On a phone the map (about 600 px, no labels) comes before any Algarve news, and the Algarve grid is 9 cards of text.
4. **National and International list order.** Numbered 1 to 8, which reads as a ranking, but the order is not explained and is not by date: three "Yesterday" items, then "23 Nov", then "Yesterday" again. A London Tube strike (tagged Europe) is at number 5 on a Portuguese site, and the list's heading says International while the tags are Portugal, Analysis and Europe, nothing beyond Europe. Under the list, "From the summit" links names from Web Summit, which ended on 12 Nov: a leftover. On a phone this list is about 4000 px down, below the map, the Algarve grid, a box of 24 figures ("By the numbers") and the "Companies we follow" box.
5. **The empty right half of the lead band on desktop: confirmed.** From the lead's text (about 620 px) to the right edge there is a dark green block about 600 px wide and 370 px high with nothing in it, while the "Now in the Algarve" ticker, the map and the rest sit below. A founder reads it as a missing image. It is the loudest thing on the page.

## Other things the readers hit
- "FROM THE ALGARVE" opens with three stories that are not about the Algarve (the ATM mark-up, the payment desk, "Who wrote this, who checked it, and who signs it": Consumers, AI, Opinion; 17 Nov). The journalist reader took the column's name at its word and read them as Algarve news.
- "By the numbers": 24 figures, with "17 Algarve towns" next to "17 district capitals so far" and "35 places": the founder could not tell what is counted. "565 gaps: looked for, not found" is honest and the journalist liked it, but the word "gaps" is jargon here.
- "1 airline flights near Faro at 02:27 UTC": number and noun disagree, and the time is the build's, not now.
- The masthead chip says "Pay for what you read . 4.99" on desktop and "Credit 4.99" on a phone (changed since my last read: the phone now has the word; N1 is mostly answered, though desktop says "Pay" and phone says "Credit").
- Every card carries "agent-written . signed": clear, consistent, and a plus for the journalist. "Signed" is still not explained on the page (F28 in my ledger).

## What works
Clear lead, one headline, a plain button. Every card says agent-written and signed. Dates present nearly everywhere. The big green numbers in the list read fast. No sideways scroll. The Algarve intro sentence ("Every Algarve town has its own agent, researching in the open") tells a cold reader what the page is.
